Night, Mainz 1492, a candlelit stone scriptorium at three in the morning. The young scribe from the image (dark curly hair tied back, short beard, tired eyes, ink-stained fingers, a thin vermilion thread on his left wrist) is checking his hand copy against the exemplar book, his left fingertip held on one line. This is the moment he finds a mistake in his own copy.
0.0-1.2 s: his fingertip slides slowly along the line on the vellum; his eyes follow it, lips pressed together; the candle flame trembles.
1.2-2.0 s: his finger stops. He freezes. His eyes flick to the exemplar book on the right, then back to his page: the words do not match.
2.0-3.4 s: he exhales through his nose, closes his eyes for a second, jaw tight, exhausted; the quill in his right hand lowers to the desk.
3.4-4.6 s: he opens his eyes and slowly lifts his head; something behind the camera has caught his attention, as if someone in the dark doorway said his name.
4.6-6.0 s: he holds still, looking just past the lens into the darkness, guilty, caught, the candle light on half his face.
